## 2026-10-20 08:45 IST · li02 · Doc carousel - India's AI buildout is a capex story first

Format: document · Pillar: Sector deep dive · Assets: media/out/posts/li02/

```
India's AI story is a capex story before it is a software story.

~1.4 GW of data-centre IT capacity in 2025. Industry estimates: ~4-5 GW by 2030. At ₹45-60 Cr per MW, that's a very physical wave of spending.

What's interesting is where the money lands first:

• Grid, transformers and switchgear - orders placed before construction
• Backup power - UPS and diesel gensets
• Cooling - chillers, CRAC units, towers
• Cabling and fiber

And the binding constraint isn't demand. It's reliable power, grid connections, water for cooling and access to imported GPUs.
```

## 2026-10-21 08:45 IST · li03 · The EV profit pool isn't at the OEM

Format: text+image · Pillar: Sector insight · Assets: media/out/posts/li03/

```
The most visible companies in a theme are rarely where the margin is.

India's electric car market:
• ~2 lakh units in FY26, ~4.5% of car sales (from ~1% in FY23)
• The battery is 35-40% of an EV's cost
• >90% of cells are still imported

Now look at margins along the chain. Drivetrain and power electronics: 15-25% EBITDA. Vehicle OEMs on their EV lines: thin to negative.

The value is accruing first to suppliers localizing what's imported today - cells, motors, power electronics - and to incumbent auto-component makers retooling machining, wiring and thermal lines for EV platforms.

Incumbents retool; they don't get replaced. That's the part most 'EV stock' lists miss.
```

## 2026-10-22 08:45 IST · li04 · ALMM - a list, not a subsidy

Format: text+image · Pillar: Policy explained · Assets: media/out/posts/li04/

```
One of the most effective industrial policies in India right now isn't a subsidy. It's a list.

ALMM - the Approved List of Models and Manufacturers - says government-linked solar projects can only buy from MNRE-approved domestic makers.

• List-I: modules. >100 GW is now enlisted - more than enough.
• List-II: cells, from June 2026. Domestic cell capacity is the scarce part.
• List-III (proposed): wafers.

The effect: the profit pool moves upstream in phases. Cells earn 25-30%+ EBITDA today; module assembly earns 10-15%.

Demand was never the question - India added a record ~36 GW of solar in FY26. ALMM decides who supplies it.

Policy that redirects demand rather than subsidizing it is worth studying closely.
```

## 2026-10-24 08:45 IST · li06 · Capacity vs generation

Format: text+image · Pillar: Myth vs data · Assets: media/out/posts/li06/

```
Two true statements about India's power sector:

1. Over 50% of installed capacity is now non-fossil - crossed in mid-2025, about five years early.
2. Coal still generates ~73-75% of the electricity.

The gap is capacity vs generation. A solar plant produces for part of the day; a coal plant runs round the clock.

Which is why the layer to watch is storage - batteries and pumped hydro. It's what lets renewables keep scaling without leaning on coal for every evening peak. Peak demand is already 250 GW+, and CEA expects ~366 GW by FY32.

Every generation method is adding capacity at once. That's rare - and it shows up across every equipment supplier in the chain.
```

## 2026-10-26 08:45 IST · li08 · Doc carousel - who builds India's warships

Format: document · Pillar: Sector deep dive · Assets: media/out/posts/li08/

```
~60 warships are under construction in Indian yards right now. The Navy's goal is 175-200 ships by ~2035, up from ~140.

A warship is a supply chain:
• Hull steel - SAIL is the sole domestic producer of DMR-249 warship-grade steel
• Propulsion and machinery
• Weapons and ammunition
• Sensors and electronics - the highest-margin layer (20-30% EBITDA)
• Shipyards that integrate it all

The constraint isn't demand. It's execution: a handful of qualified yards, limited dry docks, and multi-year build cycles that tie up capacity per vessel.
```

## 2026-10-27 08:45 IST · li09 · MRO - the under-built profit pool

Format: text+image · Pillar: Sector insight · Assets: media/out/posts/li09/

```
Indian airlines have ~1,700+ aircraft on order - one of the largest order books in the world.

Yet ~85% of India's aircraft maintenance, repair and overhaul (MRO) spend goes abroad, mostly for engines and components.

That's a profit pool that should be onshore and largely isn't yet. Engine MRO alone is ~45% of civil MRO spend.

Safran opening a LEAP engine MRO facility in Hyderabad (Nov 2025) is the kind of signal to watch: global OEMs building sustainment capacity where the fleet is growing.

Meanwhile defence exports went from ₹9,115 Cr (FY20) to ₹23,622 Cr (FY25), with a ₹50,000 Cr target for FY29. Two demand engines, one supply chain.
```

## 2026-10-28 08:45 IST · li10 · Railways - execution, not demand, sets returns

Format: text+image · Pillar: Sector insight · Assets: media/out/posts/li10/

```
Indian Railways capex: ~₹2.65 lakh Cr budgeted for FY26 - roughly 4x FY14.

But the composition is changing:
• ~99% of broad gauge is already electrified
• Wagon and EPC order inflow slowed through 2025-26
• Kavach covers under 5% of ~69,000 route km
• The first Vande Bharat sleeper entered service in Jan 2026

So the money is moving from civil works toward trainsets, signaling and components. And the margin spread is wide: signaling 20-30% EBITDA vs track & electrification EPC at 5-10%.

In a sector where the budget is large and sticky, execution and order flow - not demand - decide returns.
```

## 2026-10-29 08:45 IST · li11 · Doc carousel - packaging before wafers

Format: document · Pillar: Sector deep dive · Assets: media/out/posts/li11/

```
India's semiconductor build-out is happening in the opposite order to what most people expect.

Not wafers first. Packaging first.

• Chip demand: ~$45-50 bn (2024), >90% imported
• ISM first phase: ₹76,000 Cr; 10 fab/OSAT projects approved (~₹1.6 lakh Cr)
• Incentives cover ~70-75% of capex

OSAT/ATMP (assembly, testing, packaging) plants ramp faster and need less capital than a fab. The first India-made chips were showcased at Semicon India in Sep 2025.

And while fabs take years to reach yield, the picks and shovels get paid during construction: specialty gases and chemicals (20-30% EBITDA), cleanroom HVAC, ultra-pure water, design services.
```

## 2026-10-31 08:45 IST · li13 · Telecom - monetize > build

Format: text+image · Pillar: Sector insight · Assets: media/out/posts/li13/

```
India built ~5 lakh 5G base stations in roughly three years - one of the fastest rollouts globally. ~39 Cr 5G subscribers by 2025.

That build phase has peaked. The next phase is different:

• Operators: ARPU repair (the industry talks about ₹300) in a market that's now a profitable duopoly-plus
• Towers & passive infra: ~50% EBITDA, pulled by densification
• Fiber: needed for every new small cell and FWA home
• EPC installers: 8-12% EBITDA, more exposed to falling capex

Same sector, two very different cycles depending on which layer you look at.
```

# The Map #2: Ships, jets and trains (outline)

*Diwali edition, Sun 8 Nov 2026, 10:00 IST. Three Make-in-India hardware supply chains where execution, not demand, sets the pace.*

Draft this in fortnight 2. It reuses the week-2 carousels (ig15, ig17, ig19) as figures.

## Structure

1. **Opening.** Defence production hit a record ₹1.51 lakh Cr in FY25 (+18% YoY), and railway capex is about 4x FY14. Both have more demand than capacity.
2. **Naval: ~60 ships in the yards.**
   - Fleet goal: 175-200 ships by ~2035, up from ~140.
   - Value chain and margins (figure: ig15-03). Sensors and electronics earn 20-30%; yards earn 10-20%.
   - Bottleneck: dry docks and slipways. P-75I is the swing award to watch.
3. **Aerospace: two demand engines.**
   - Defence exports went from ₹9,115 Cr to ₹23,622 Cr (FY20-FY25), against a target of ₹50,000 Cr by FY29 (figure: ig17-02).
   - About 85% of MRO spend goes abroad, so MRO is the under-built profit pool.
   - Qualification is the moat: engine components earn 25-35% EBITDA.
4. **Railways: the mix shifts.**
   - Capex of ₹2.65 lakh Cr (FY26 BE), but about 99% of broad gauge is already electrified.
   - Kavach covers under 5% of route km. Signaling earns 20-30%, while EPC earns 5-10% (figure: ig19-04).
5. **Hidden connections.** Bharat Forge sits in all three chains (plus EVs), and BEL in naval, aerospace and telecom (figure: ig24-02).
6. **What to watch in Q2 FY27 results.** Order books and order inflow, execution and revenue conversion, working capital at the defence PSUs, and new Vande Bharat and Kavach awards.

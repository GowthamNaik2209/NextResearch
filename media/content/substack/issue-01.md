# The Map #1: AI's physical supply chain in India

*Data-centre capacity is set to roughly triple by 2030. The first rupees go to transformers, gensets and chillers, not chips.*

![The Map, Issue #1](../../out/posts/ss01/ss01-01.png)

Welcome to **The Map**, a fortnightly newsletter from NextResearch. Each issue takes one Indian supply chain, walks it layer by layer, and shows where the money, margins and bottlenecks sit. We don't give stock tips. We give you the map, the numbers and the questions worth asking.

This first issue covers the theme everyone is talking about and few have actually mapped: **AI infrastructure in India.**

---

## 1. The big picture: a capex story before a software story

India had about **1.4 GW** of installed data-centre IT capacity in 2025. Industry estimates, including the AI campuses already announced, put it at **~4-5 GW by 2030**.

![Installed data-centre IT capacity in India](../../out/posts/ig03/ig03-02.png)

Three things are driving this:

- **Cloud and AI workloads.** Hyperscalers have pledged $65 bn+ for India. Google's $15 bn AI hub in Visakhapatnam (Oct 2025) and expanded pledges from Microsoft and Amazon (Dec 2025) are the headline numbers.
- **Data localization.** The DPDP Rules (notified Nov 2025) and RBI's payment-data rules keep more data, and the compute that processes it, inside India.
- **Sovereign AI compute.** IndiaAI's GPU programme counts 30,000+ GPUs.

Building one megawatt of capacity costs roughly **₹45-60 Cr**: the shell, the electricals and the cooling. Multiply that by 3+ GW of new capacity and you get a construction cycle measured in lakh crores, and most of it is not spent on chips.

## 2. The value chain, layer by layer

Here is how a data-centre campus breaks down, with typical margins at each layer:

![Data-centre value chain with typical margins](../../out/posts/ig03/ig03-03.png)

| Layer | What it does | Typical margin | Moat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grid, transformers & switchgear | Steps grid power down and distributes it | 12-20% EBITDA | High |
| Backup power | UPS and diesel gensets for outages | 15-20% EBITDA | Medium |
| Cooling | Chillers, CRAC units, cooling towers | 8-12% EBITDA | Medium |
| Cabling & fiber | Power and data cabling, backbone links | 10-14% EBITDA | Low |
| Compute & distribution | Servers, GPU systems, IT distribution | Low single digit to ~15% | Medium |
| Operators & landlords | Own the campus, lease the capacity | 40-50% EBITDA | High |

Two things stand out. First, **the earliest orders go to electrical, backup and cooling suppliers**, often before the building is finished, so their order books show the cycle first. Second, **operators earn the fattest margins, but India has few listed pure-play operators**. Most listed exposure is indirect, through diversified parents (Bharti Airtel's Nxtra, for example).

## 3. The constraint nobody prices in

Demand is not the bottleneck. These are:

- **Power and grid connections.** How much compute a site can host depends on how much power it can draw, and grid-interconnection approvals take time.
- **Water.** Evaporative cooling is water-hungry, and several data-centre hubs are in water-stressed regions. Liquid cooling for dense AI racks changes the equation, but it is still early.
- **GPUs.** Global accelerator supply and export controls sit largely outside India's control.
- **Concentration.** About **50%** of capacity is in Mumbai. Rentals there are the first place oversupply would show up.

## 4. Three companies, three layers

These are examples to make the map concrete. They are not recommendations. Figures are company seed financials as of 25 Sep 2026; open the live card on the site for current numbers.

![Cummins India on NextResearch](../../out/raw/stockcard-cumminsind.png)

- **Cummins India** (backup power). Diesel generator sets used for data-centre power backup. Revenue roughly doubled from ₹6,171 Cr (FY22) to ₹12,143 Cr (FY26); ROCE 39.5%. Watch for: diesel emission-norm transitions, which raise genset costs, and how long backup has to run as grids come under stress.
- **Polycab India** (cabling). Structured and power cabling for data-centre fit-outs, alongside a much larger wires and cables business. Revenue grew from ₹12,204 Cr to ₹28,884 Cr over FY22-FY26. Watch for: copper price swings, which flow straight into cable costs, and fit-outs that lag shell construction.
- **Netweb Technologies** (compute). HPC and AI servers and GPU systems. Revenue grew about 90% in the latest year (₹1,149 Cr to ₹2,184 Cr). Watch for: GPU allocation, and how much of that growth repeats.

Notice how different these businesses are. One theme, three very different demand drivers, margin structures and risks. That is the point of mapping the layer before looking at the stock.

## 5. What to watch as Q2 FY27 results come in

As companies report the July-September quarter over the next few weeks, these are the signals that move the data-centre map:

1. **Order inflow** at transformer, switchgear and genset makers that is tagged to data centres.
2. **Hyperscaler capex guidance** in global results, and any India-specific mentions.
3. **New campus and land announcements**, especially outside Mumbai.
4. **Grid-connection and power-purchase approvals** for announced campuses.
5. **GPU allocations** to Indian cloud providers under IndiaAI.
